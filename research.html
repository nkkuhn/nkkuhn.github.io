The lab is especially interested in dendrites. Dendrites are the tree-like structures through which neurons receive thousands of inputs. Rather than acting as passive cables, dendrites can filter, amplify, suppress, and combine incoming signals through their morphology and ion channels. We study how these dendritic computations influence the activity of defined neural circuits and help animals identify which signals are relevant.

To connect dendrites, circuits, and behavior, we combine molecular mapping, microscopy, slice electrophysiology, in vivo recordings, behavioral analysis, and computational modeling. We use genetic tools to target specific neuron types and manipulate selected ion channels, allowing us to follow how changes in membrane proteins and dendritic processing affect neural circuit dynamics and visually guided actions.

In the long term, we aim to build predictive models that link multiple scales of brain function: from ion channels and dendrites, to neurons, circuits, and behavior. This will help us understand how small cellular changes can lead to adaptive decisions or maladaptive responses, and may also inspire new ideas for energy-efficient computation based on the graded processing power of dendrites.
