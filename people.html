Norma Kühn (PI): I am an F.R.S.-FNRS Research Associate at GIGA Neurosciences, University of Liège, where I lead a lab focused on Dendrites, Circuits and Behavior. My research investigates how dendritic processing in the superior colliculus, a major sensory-motor hub, shapes the transformation of visual information into rapid and flexible actions. My scientific path began in theoretical physics at Leipzig University, Germany, where I developed a strong interest in quantitative approaches to complex systems. I then moved into neuroscience during my PhD in the lab of Tim Gollisch at the University Medical Center Göttingen, where I studied multiplexed spike codes in retinal ganglion cells and how visual features can be recovered from population activity. As a postdoctoral fellow in Karl Farrow’s Neural Circuits of Vision group at NERF/KU Leuven, I expanded this work from the retina to visual pathways controlling innate orienting and defensive behaviors. Combining viral tracing, multiphoton imaging, electrophysiology, behavior and computational modeling, I investigated how visual information is organized across retinal inputs, integrated by genetically identified superior colliculus neurons, and modified by brain-wide inputs to support flexible behavior. My current lab builds on this background to understand how dendrites contribute to sensory-motor computation. We ask how dendritic structure and ion channel composition allow neurons to filter, combine and transform incoming signals, and how these cellular mechanisms shape circuit activity and behavior. The long-term goal is to link dendrites, circuits and behavior across scales, from membrane proteins and single-neuron computations to adaptive and maladaptive responses.
I was supported by a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Postdoctoral Fellowship (project FeaGatSu) and by an FWO Senior Postdoctoral Fellowship before starting my independent group at the University of Liège.
